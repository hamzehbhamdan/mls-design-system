# Inclusive language

## Soccer For All

MLS's stated position. Note the styling: capital F on "For", per the league's own page. Hashtag `#SoccerForAll`. Programme vehicle: **MLS WORKS** (all caps).

Mission statement, verbatim:

> Major League Soccer is dedicated to supporting the communities where we live and play our games, and to providing an environment in which our staff, players, partners and fans are treated with dignity and respect.

The canonical enumeration of protected categories, and the single most quotable line for a brand-language reference:

> Soccer For All signifies that everyone is welcome to MLS, regardless of **race, color, religion, national origin, gender, gender identity, disability, sexual orientation or socioeconomic status**.

Commemorative moments MLS names explicitly: Black History Month, Women's History Month, Asian & Pacific Islander Heritage Month, LGBTQ+ Pride Month, Hispanic Heritage Month. The league uses **"LGBTQ+"**.

Accessibility vocabulary MLS uses: *sensory rooms*, *mini-pitch installs*, and "first-team experiences for individuals with and without intellectual and physical disabilities."

## Fan Code of Conduct

> Major League Soccer and each of its Clubs are committed to creating a safe, comfortable, and enjoyable match-day experience for all fans visiting MLS stadiums and facilities.

Key prohibited-conduct language:

> Displaying signs, symbols, images, using language or making gestures that are threatening, abusive, or discriminatory

> Displaying signs, symbols or images for commercial purposes or for electioneering, campaigning or advocating

**Two things worth flagging honestly:**

**1. The two documents do not use the same protected-category list.** The Code says *race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, gender, gender identity, ability, and/or sexual orientation*. Soccer For All says *race, color, religion, national origin, gender, gender identity, disability, sexual orientation or socioeconomic status*. The Code has *ethnicity* and *ability*; Soccer For All has *color*, *disability* and *socioeconomic status*. If you are building a controlled vocabulary, this is a real inconsistency you will have to resolve rather than inherit.

**2. The Code uses "fans" throughout, never "supporters."** Deliberate, and consistent with the two-tier distinction in [08 Voice](08-voice-and-tone.md).

Note also **"match-day" hyphenated** here — a third variant alongside "matchday" and "gameday."

## Latino terminology

**MLS has published no league-level inclusive-language style guidance.** The only substantive published guidance in the ecosystem is Atlanta United's, and it is genuinely useful:

**Latino over Hispanic.** The club prioritises *Latino* and celebrates *"Latin Heritage, not Hispanic Heritage"*, on the stated grounds that most people from Latin American countries do not identify with "Hispanic."

**On pan-ethnic labels:** *"Most Latin-Americans prefer their family's country of origin over pan-ethnic terms."* Use *Latino* only when a broader label is genuinely necessary; prefer the specific nationality.

**Spanish-language terms for Black communities**, from the same guide:

| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| *afroestadounidense* | Black U.S. community |
| *afroamericano/a* | Black / African American |
| *afrodescendiente* | Black person — the most inclusive descriptor |
| *afrolatino/a* | Latinos of African descent |

**This conflicts with the league**, which uses "Hispanic Heritage Month" — the federal name — on its own Soccer For All page. Both positions are defensible. Pick one deliberately.

## Working guidance

Beyond what MLS publishes, these follow from the league's stated commitments and from general practice. Marked as extrapolation, not as MLS policy.

**Players.** Identify by position and club, not by nationality, unless nationality is relevant to the story. "Designated Player" is a roster category, not a description of a person — do not use it as a synonym for "foreign player," which it is not.

**Disability.** MLS's own Code uses *ability* rather than *disability* in its list, which is identity-neutral framing. When writing about accessibility programmes, follow the league's concrete vocabulary — sensory rooms, mini-pitch installs — rather than abstractions about inclusion.

**Gender.** MLS is a men's league operating alongside NWSL and club-affiliated women's sides. Do not use "soccer" to mean "men's soccer" by default where both exist in the same context. Where a club runs both, name both.

**Supporters' groups.** Autonomous entities. Do not speak for them, do not casually tag or appropriate them, and do not flatten them into "fan engagement." See the caution in [08 Voice](08-voice-and-tone.md) — this vocabulary is politically loaded, not neutral.

**Accessibility is part of inclusion.** A brand that publishes a Soccer For All commitment and ships a product at 2:1 contrast has a consistency problem. See [12 Accessibility](12-accessibility.md).
