# Brand foundation

## The league

Major League Soccer is the top-division professional soccer league of the United States and Canada. Founded 1996. Thirty clubs, fifteen in each conference, as of the 2026 season. Headquartered in New York City. 2026 is the league's thirty-first season.

Standard boilerplate, reproduced as MLS writes it and updated annually:

> Headquartered in New York City, Major League Soccer -- celebrating its 31st season in 2026 -- features 30 clubs throughout the United States and Canada.

Note the double hyphen. That is MLS's own press-release convention, not a typo.

## The three pillars

The three stars in the crest are not decoration. MLS's own launch explanation is unambiguous:

> The three stars represent the pillars of our brand: **For Club, For Country, For Community**.

These are the spine of the identity, and they are a useful test for any piece of work. Something that serves none of the three is probably off-brand.

**For Club.** The thirty clubs are the primary emotional unit, not the league. This is why the crest is colourable, why supporter culture is credited rather than claimed, and why this design system makes club theming a first-class mechanism instead of a skin.

**For Country.** MLS represents two nations, and the palette is explicitly a reference to both. Canadian clubs are not a regional variant — CF Montréal is French-first, and the Canadian Championship sits alongside the U.S. Open Cup rather than beneath it.

**For Community.** Expressed programmatically through MLS WORKS and Soccer For All, and linguistically in how the league writes about supporters' groups: as autonomous entities that built something the league benefits from, not as an audience it created.

## Positioning, 2026

MLS is at a genuine inflection point, and the framing is worth understanding because it explains the current tone.

The league's post-World Cup campaign, launched July 2026 during FOX's semifinal and final coverage and billed by MLS as the largest coordinated campaign in its history, is **"Thanks World, We'll Take It From Here."** The commissioner's letter is explicit about how to read it:

> For MLS, "We'll take it from here" is not a slogan. It is a responsibility.

That is a claim of inheritance: MLS positioning itself as the permanent domestic destination for the audience the 2026 World Cup temporarily created. It pairs with the calendar move to form a single narrative — *we are now a real global league* — and it is precisely why the crest did not need to change. MLS is reframing meaning rather than redrawing marks.

Alongside it sits a striking piece of honesty from the outgoing commissioner:

> We've got to win Concacaf consistently. We've got to get into the Club World Cup, and get out of the early rounds.

Commercial success is treated as achieved. Sporting standing is treated as aspirational and openly unfinished. **This split is the most useful single thing to internalise about MLS's voice**, and it generalises into a rule: celebrate the match maximally, describe the league plainly.

## Brand personality

Derived from observed output rather than from a published personality statement, so treat it as characterisation rather than doctrine.

| MLS is | MLS is not |
|---|---|
| **Ascendant** — the slash drives upward; the language is about trajectory, not arrival | Complacent, or claiming a status it has not earned |
| **Plural** — two countries, thirty clubs, a genuinely multilingual audience | Monolithic; the league brand never overwrites a club's |
| **Fluent in the global game** — match, pitch, kit, golazo, tifo, unglossed | Insular, or translating world football into American sports vocabulary |
| **Self-aware** — willing to be funny at its own expense in editorial | Po-faced, or humourless about its own hype |
| **Credit-giving** — supporter culture is named as something supporters built | Appropriating; the league does not claim what it did not make |

## Naming the league

- **MLS** on second reference, and in nearly all UI. No periods.
- **Major League Soccer** on first reference in formal writing.
- **Never "the MLS."** No definite article, in English. In Spanish, *la MLS* is acceptable mid-sentence but not at the start of a sentence or a headline.
- **`MLS'`**, not `MLS's`, for the possessive.

## What this means for design work

1. **Design for the club, brand for the league.** If a surface belongs to a club, the club's colour should dominate and the league mark should be present but subordinate. The crest system was built for exactly this.
2. **Motion goes up and to the right.** The slash has a stated direction. Transitions, reveals and emphasis should follow it rather than fight it.
3. **Do not over-claim in copy.** The brand's own voice is more restrained about the league's standing than most sports marketing. Matching that restraint reads as confident; exceeding it reads as insecure.
4. **Leave room for the supporter.** Tifo, capos, away support and supporters' groups are treated as a culture the league hosts. Design that flattens them into "fan engagement" is off-brand in a way that is hard to articulate but immediately obvious to the audience.
